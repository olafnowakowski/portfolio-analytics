# Pre-Cleaning Activities and Data Insufficiency Notes

## Main Idea

Before data cleaning begins, analysts should complete **pre-cleaning activities**.

Pre-cleaning helps analysts understand the data, check whether it is reliable, and confirm that it supports the business objective.

These steps are important because cleaning data without understanding it first can lead to inaccurate analysis.

---

# 1. Why Pre-Cleaning Matters

Pre-cleaning activities help analysts prepare data before making changes to it.

Before analyzing or cleaning data, analysts should:

- check data integrity
- connect the data to the business objective
- identify whether the data is sufficient
- decide when to stop collecting data
- recognize limitations in the dataset

---

# 2. Step 1: Review Data Integrity

Before analysis, analysts need to understand the data they are working with.

Data integrity means the data is:

| Quality | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | The data is correct |
| Consistency | The data matches across sources or systems |
| Completeness | The data has all necessary information |

---

## Tasks Before Analyzing Data

Before analyzing data, analysts should:

1. Determine data integrity by assessing accuracy, consistency, and completeness.
2. Connect objectives to data by checking how the business objective can be served by the data.
3. Know when to stop collecting data.

---

# 3. Step 2: Identify What Makes Data Insufficient

One goal of pre-cleaning is to identify insufficient data.

**Insufficient data** means the data is not enough or not appropriate for the analysis.

Data may be insufficient if it:

- comes from only one source
- continuously updates and is incomplete
- is outdated
- is geographically limited

---

# 4. Examples of Insufficient Data

| Issue | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| One source only | The data may not show the full picture |
| Continuously updating | The data may not be complete yet |
| Outdated data | The data may no longer reflect the current situation |
| Geographically limited | The data may not apply to other locations or populations |

---

# 5. Step 3: Deal With Insufficient Data

When data is insufficient, analysts should not force conclusions.

Instead, they can:

- identify trends within the available data
- wait for more data if time allows
- discuss the issue with stakeholders
- adjust the business objective
- search for a new dataset

---

# 6. Why Analysts Should Communicate With Stakeholders

If the data does not fully support the business objective, analysts should talk to stakeholders.

This helps determine whether to:

- collect more data
- use a different dataset
- change the scope of the analysis
- adjust the objective
- explain limitations in the final report

---

# 7. Reflection Question 1

## Why are pre-cleaning steps important to complete prior to data cleaning?

Pre-cleaning steps are important because they help analysts understand the data before changing it. By checking data integrity, connecting the data to the business objective, and identifying insufficiencies, analysts can avoid cleaning the wrong data or preparing data that does not support the analysis.

---

# 8. Reflection Question 2

## What problems might occur if you don’t follow these steps?

If these steps are skipped, the analysis may be inaccurate or misleading. The data could be incomplete, outdated, duplicated, or unrelated to the business objective. This can lead to poor conclusions, wasted time, incorrect recommendations, and decisions based on data that is not reliable.

---

# 9. Common Mistakes to Avoid

## Mistake 1: Cleaning data before understanding it

Do not start cleaning until you understand the dataset and its purpose.

## Mistake 2: Ignoring data integrity

If the data is inaccurate, inconsistent, or incomplete, the analysis may be unreliable.

## Mistake 3: Using data that does not match the business objective

Even clean data is not useful if it does not answer the business question.

## Mistake 4: Ignoring insufficient data

If the dataset is too limited, outdated, incomplete, or geographically narrow, conclusions may be weak.

## Mistake 5: Failing to communicate limitations

Stakeholders need to know when data limitations affect the analysis.

---

# 10. Key Takeaways

- Pre-cleaning happens before data cleaning.
- Pre-cleaning helps analysts understand the data.
- Data integrity should be checked before analysis.
- Data integrity includes accuracy, consistency, and completeness.
- Data should align with the business objective.
- Insufficient data can weaken analysis.
- Data may be insufficient if it comes from one source, is incomplete, outdated, or geographically limited.
- Analysts can handle insufficient data by identifying trends, waiting for more data, adjusting the objective, or finding a new dataset.
- Skipping pre-cleaning can lead to inaccurate conclusions and poor recommendations.

---

# Final Summary

Pre-cleaning activities help analysts prepare for data cleaning and analysis. Before cleaning data, analysts should review data integrity, connect the data to the business objective, and identify whether the data is sufficient. Data integrity means the data is accurate, consistent, and complete. Data may be insufficient if it comes from only one source, is still updating, is outdated, or is geographically limited. When data is insufficient, analysts can look for trends, wait for more data, discuss changes with stakeholders, adjust the objective, or search for a new dataset. The main lesson is that pre-cleaning helps prevent inaccurate analysis, wasted time, and poor business decisions.
