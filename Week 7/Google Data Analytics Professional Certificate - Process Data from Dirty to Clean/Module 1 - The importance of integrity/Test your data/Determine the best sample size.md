# Sample Size, Confidence Level, and Margin of Error

## Main Idea

Sample size is important because it helps analysts study a smaller part of a population instead of analyzing the entire population.

Using a sample is usually faster, cheaper, and more practical than collecting data from everyone.

A good sample can still lead to valid and useful findings if it represents the larger population well.

---

# 1. What Is Sample Size?

**Sample size** is the number of people, records, or data points included in a sample.

A sample is a smaller part of a larger population.

The sample should represent the population as accurately as possible.

---

# 2. Why Businesses Use Samples

Businesses use samples because analyzing an entire population can be:

- expensive
- time-consuming
- difficult to manage
- unnecessary for some studies

Instead of collecting data from everyone, businesses can analyze a smaller group and use the results to make conclusions about the larger population.

---

# 3. Sample Size and Business Objectives

Sample size helps businesses test whether their data supports a business objective.

For example, a business might use a sample to test:

- customer preferences
- product interest
- survey results
- marketing effectiveness
- satisfaction levels
- sales expectations

---

# 4. Sample Size Calculators

A **sample size calculator** helps determine how large a sample should be.

To use a sample size calculator, you usually need to input:

- population size
- confidence level
- margin of error

The calculator then estimates the minimum sample size needed for the study.

---

# 5. Population Size

**Population size** is the total number of people, records, or data values in the full group being studied.

## Example

If a middle school has 500 students, then the population size is:

```text
500
```

---

# 6. Confidence Level

**Confidence level** is the probability that your sample accurately reflects the larger population.

In simple terms, it shows how confident you are that the sample results represent the full population.

---

## Common Confidence Levels

Many industries use confidence levels such as:

```text
90%
95%
99%
```

A 99% confidence level is very strong, but many studies use 90% or 95%.

---

# 7. Confidence Level by Industry

Different industries may need different confidence levels.

## High-Stakes Example

Pharmaceutical companies usually want a very high confidence level because they test medicines and need to know whether they are safe and effective.

## Lower-Stakes Example

A paint company testing new colors may be comfortable with a lower confidence level because the decision is less risky.

---

# 8. Margin of Error

**Margin of error** tells you how close your sample results are expected to be to the results you would get from the entire population.

A smaller margin of error means the sample results are expected to be closer to the true population result.

---

# 9. Confidence Level and Margin of Error Are Independent

Confidence level and margin of error do not need to add up to 100%.

They are separate concepts.

## Example

You can have:

```text
95% confidence level
5% margin of error
```

or:

```text
95% confidence level
3% margin of error
```

The second option requires a larger sample size because the margin of error is smaller.

---

# 10. Example: Middle School Candy Preferences

A principal wants to study students’ candy preferences.

The school has:

```text
500 students
```

The principal wants:

```text
95% confidence level
5% margin of error
```

Using a sample size calculator, the recommended sample size is about:

```text
218 students
```

This means the school should survey at least 218 students to meet those criteria.

---

# 11. Interpreting the Result

If the school surveys 218 students and finds that 55% prefer chocolate, the analyst can be reasonably confident that this result reflects the full population of 500 students.

The sample size of 218 is the minimum needed based on:

- population size of 500
- confidence level of 95%
- margin of error of 5%

---

# 12. What Happens When Margin of Error Changes?

If the margin of error gets smaller, the sample size must get larger.

## Example

With a population of 500 students:

| Confidence Level | Margin of Error | Approximate Sample Size |
|---|---|---|
| 95% | 5% | 218 |
| 95% | 3% | 341 |

A 3% margin of error requires more survey responses because the results need to be more precise.

---

# 13. Relationship Between Sample Size and Accuracy

In general:

- larger sample size = more representative results
- smaller margin of error = larger sample size needed
- higher confidence level = larger sample size needed

A larger sample helps improve the reliability of the study.

---

# 14. Why Sample Size Supports Data Integrity

Sample size connects to data integrity because the sample needs to be reliable and representative.

If the sample is too small or poorly chosen, the analysis may not accurately reflect the population.

This can weaken the integrity of the analysis.

---

# 15. Common Mistakes to Avoid

## Mistake 1: Using too small of a sample

A sample that is too small may not represent the population well.

## Mistake 2: Ignoring margin of error

A large margin of error means results may be less precise.

## Mistake 3: Assuming confidence level and margin of error add up to 100%

They are independent and should be treated separately.

## Mistake 4: Using the same sample size for every study

Sample size depends on the population, confidence level, margin of error, and business objective.

## Mistake 5: Forgetting the business risk

Higher-risk decisions usually require stronger confidence and larger samples.

---

# 16. Key Questions to Ask

Before choosing a sample size, ask:

- What is the population size?
- What confidence level is needed?
- What margin of error is acceptable?
- How accurate do the results need to be?
- What is the business objective?
- How risky is the decision?
- Can a sample size calculator help?

---

# 17. Key Takeaways

- Sample size is a smaller part of a population used for analysis.
- Samples help businesses avoid the cost and time of analyzing an entire population.
- A sample should represent the larger population.
- Sample size calculators help determine the minimum sample size needed.
- Sample size calculators usually require population size, confidence level, and margin of error.
- Confidence level shows how likely the sample is to reflect the population.
- Margin of error shows how close the sample result is expected to be to the population result.
- Higher confidence levels usually require larger samples.
- Smaller margins of error require larger samples.
- Confidence level and margin of error are independent of each other.
- Sample size affects the reliability and integrity of analysis.

---

# Final Summary

Sample size helps analysts study a representative part of a larger population. This is useful because analyzing an entire population can be expensive and time-consuming. To calculate sample size, analysts often use a sample size calculator and input the population size, confidence level, and margin of error. Confidence level is the probability that the sample accurately reflects the population, while margin of error shows how close the sample result is expected to be to the full population result. In the middle school candy example, a population of 500 students with a 95% confidence level and 5% margin of error requires a sample size of about 218 students. If the margin of error is reduced to 3%, the sample size increases to about 341. The main lesson is that larger samples usually create more reliable results, especially when higher confidence or lower margin of error is needed.
