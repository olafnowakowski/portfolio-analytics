# Data Ethics

## Main Idea

**Data ethics** is about using data responsibly.

It includes standards of right and wrong that guide how data is:

- collected
- shared
- stored
- processed
- analyzed
- used

Data ethics matters because data can affect real people and real decisions.

---

# What Is Ethics?

## Definition

**Ethics** refers to well-founded standards of right and wrong that guide what people should do.

Ethics often involves:

- rights
- obligations
- fairness
- benefits to society
- specific virtues
- responsible behavior

---

# Personal Ethics vs Data Ethics

## Personal Ethics

Personal ethics are the principles people use to guide their own behavior.

Examples:

- do not lie
- do not cheat
- do not steal
- treat people fairly

---

## Data Ethics

**Data ethics** refers to standards of right and wrong that guide how data is collected, shared, and used.

Data ethics is especially important because data can be collected and used at a very large scale.

---

# Why Data Ethics Matters

Data analysis creates questions, challenges, and opportunities.

Because people have conscious and subconscious biases, analysts cannot rely only on personal ethics.

They need shared standards for responsible data use.

### Important Lesson

Data ethics helps protect people from harm caused by unfair, unclear, or irresponsible data practices.

---

# Data Ethics and Bias

Bias can make data ethics difficult.

Because everyone has personal and subconscious biases, data can be collected or interpreted unfairly.

Data ethics helps reduce the risk of:

- biased outcomes
- unfair targeting
- privacy violations
- unclear data use
- misuse of personal information

---

# Data Protection Laws

Governments around the world have recognized the importance of data privacy.

They have created data protection laws to help protect people and their data.

---

## GDPR

The **General Data Protection Regulation**, or **GDPR**, is a data protection law from the European Union.

It was created to help protect people’s data privacy.

---

# Company Responsibility

Companies that collect and use data have a responsibility to protect it.

They should use data in ways that are:

- transparent
- fair
- responsible
- privacy-focused
- accountable

---

# Accountability in Data Ethics

Data ethics focuses on the accountability companies have when they collect and use data.

Companies should be able to explain:

- why data is collected
- how data is used
- who has access to it
- how it is protected
- how long it is stored
- whether people can opt out

---

# Six Aspects of Data Ethics

This lesson introduces six major aspects of data ethics:

1. **Ownership**
2. **Transaction transparency**
3. **Consent**
4. **Currency**
5. **Privacy**
6. **Openness**

This lesson focuses mainly on the first four.

---

# 1. Ownership

## Definition

**Ownership** means individuals own the raw data they provide.

The organization that collects, stores, processes, or analyzes the data does not automatically own the raw data.

---

## What Individuals Control

Individuals should have primary control over:

- how their data is used
- how their data is processed
- how their data is shared

### Important Lesson

People own the raw data they provide.

---

# 2. Transaction Transparency

## Definition

**Transaction transparency** means all data processing activities and algorithms should be explainable and understandable to the person who provides the data.

---

# Why Transaction Transparency Matters

People should understand what happens to their data after they provide it.

This includes understanding:

- how the data is processed
- what algorithms are used
- what decisions may be made from the data
- whether the process is fair
- whether the result is unbiased

---

# Transaction Transparency and Bias

Transaction transparency helps respond to concerns about data bias.

If people can understand how their data is used, they can better judge whether the outcome is fair.

They can also raise concerns if they believe the analysis is biased or unfair.

### Important Lesson

Transparent analysis helps people evaluate whether data outcomes are fair.

---

# 3. Consent

## Definition

**Consent** is an individual’s right to know how and why their data will be used before agreeing to provide it.

---

# What People Should Know Before Giving Consent

Before giving consent, people should understand:

- why the data is being collected
- how the data will be used
- how long the data will be stored
- who will use the data
- whether the data will be shared
- whether they can opt out

---

# Consent in Real Life

The best form of consent would be a clear conversation between:

- the person providing the data
- the person or organization requesting the data

However, because so much activity happens online, consent often appears as:

- a terms and conditions checkbox
- a privacy policy link
- an agreement form

---

# Problem With Online Consent

Many people do not read all the details in terms and conditions.

This can make consent less meaningful if users do not fully understand what they are agreeing to.

---

# Why Consent Matters

Consent helps prevent people and groups from being unfairly targeted.

This is especially important for marginalized groups, who are often more likely to be misrepresented by biased data.

### Important Lesson

Consent gives people the right to understand and control how their data is used.

---

# 4. Currency

## Definition

**Currency** means individuals should be aware of financial transactions that result from the use of their personal data.

---

# Why Currency Matters

If a person’s data helps fund a company’s efforts, they should know:

- what those efforts are
- how their data contributes
- whether money is being made from their data
- whether they can opt out

---

# Currency and Choice

People should have the opportunity to opt out if they do not want their personal data used in ways that create financial value for an organization.

### Important Lesson

People should know if their data is being used for financial gain.

---

# 5. Privacy

## Definition

**Privacy** refers to protecting people’s personal information and giving them control over how it is accessed and used.

---

# Why Privacy Matters

Privacy protects individuals from:

- misuse of personal information
- unwanted exposure
- unfair targeting
- identity risks
- loss of control over their data

---

# 6. Openness

## Definition

**Openness** means data practices should be transparent and accessible when appropriate.

It involves being clear about how data is collected, shared, and used.

---

# Data Ethics Summary Table

| Aspect of Data Ethics | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ownership** | Individuals own the raw data they provide |
| **Transaction Transparency** | Data processing and algorithms should be explainable |
| **Consent** | People should know how and why their data will be used before agreeing |
| **Currency** | People should know about financial use of their personal data |
| **Privacy** | Personal data should be protected |
| **Openness** | Data practices should be clear and transparent |

---

# Why Data Ethics Is Still Evolving

The ability to collect, share, and use huge amounts of data is relatively new.

Because of this, the laws and rules around data are still developing.

### Important Lesson

Data ethics is an evolving field because technology changes quickly.

---

# Data Ethics and Organizations

Organizations that use data should:

- protect personal data
- explain data practices clearly
- ask for consent
- reduce bias
- respect ownership
- allow opt-out choices when possible
- be transparent about financial uses of data

---

# Big Lesson

Data ethics helps ensure that data is used in ways that respect people’s rights, privacy, fairness, and control.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Ethics are standards of right and wrong.
- Data ethics guides how data is collected, shared, and used.
- Personal ethics are not enough because people have conscious and subconscious biases.
- Data ethics helps protect people from unfair or irresponsible data practices.
- Governments have created data protection laws to protect privacy.
- GDPR is a European Union law created to protect data privacy.
- Ownership means individuals own the raw data they provide.
- Transaction transparency means data processing and algorithms should be explainable.
- Consent means people should know how and why their data will be used before agreeing.
- Currency means people should know if their data creates financial value.
- Privacy and openness are also important parts of data ethics.
- Companies are accountable for protecting and responsibly using the data they collect.

---

# Extra Important Notes

- People should have control over how their data is used, processed, and shared.
- Transparency helps people judge whether data outcomes are fair.
- Consent should be informed, not hidden in unclear terms and conditions.
- Marginalized groups can be harmed when data is biased or used unfairly.
- Data protection laws are still evolving as technology changes.
- Responsible data use requires both technical skill and ethical judgment.

# Final Summary

Data ethics refers to the standards of right and wrong that guide how data is collected, shared, and used. It is important because data affects real people, and personal bias can make ethical decisions more difficult. Governments have created laws such as the GDPR to protect data privacy, while companies also have a responsibility to use data responsibly. This lesson introduces six aspects of data ethics: ownership, transaction transparency, consent, currency, privacy, and openness. Ownership means individuals own the raw data they provide. Transaction transparency means data processes and algorithms should be explainable. Consent means people should understand how and why their data will be used before agreeing. Currency means people should know when their data is connected to financial gain. The main lesson is that ethical data use protects people’s rights, privacy, fairness, and control over their own information.
