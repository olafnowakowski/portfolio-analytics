# Data Privacy

## Main Idea

**Data privacy** is about protecting a person’s information and activity whenever data is collected, used, shared, or stored.

Privacy is one of the most personal and important parts of data ethics.

---

# What Is Privacy?

Privacy means having control over personal information.

Different people may define privacy in different ways, but everyone is entitled to it.

Examples of privacy concerns include:

- family members wanting privacy on a shared computer
- a teenager sharing a selfie with only selected people
- a company protecting customers’ credit card information
- individuals wanting control over how their data is used

---

# What Is Data Privacy?

## Definition

**Data privacy** means preserving a data subject’s information and activity any time a data transaction occurs.

Data privacy is also sometimes called:

- information privacy
- data protection

---

# What Data Privacy Covers

Data privacy is mainly about:

- access to data
- use of data
- collection of data
- protection of data
- legal rights to data

---

# Data Subject

A **data subject** is the person whose data is being collected, used, stored, or shared.

### Example

If a company stores your email address, purchase history, or credit card information, you are the data subject.

---

# Rights Connected to Data Privacy

A person should have certain protections and rights related to their private data.

These include:

- protection from unauthorized access
- freedom from inappropriate use of data
- the right to inspect data
- the right to update data
- the right to correct data
- the ability to give consent for data use
- the legal right to access their data

---

# 1. Protection From Unauthorized Access

People should be protected from others accessing their private data without permission.

### Example

A company should protect customer credit card information from hackers or unauthorized employees.

---

# 2. Freedom From Inappropriate Use

People’s data should not be used in ways that are unfair, unexpected, or inappropriate.

### Example

A company should not use customer information for a purpose the customer did not agree to.

---

# 3. Right to Inspect Data

People should be able to see what data an organization has about them.

### Example

A customer may want to know what personal information a company has stored in its system.

---

# 4. Right to Update or Correct Data

People should be able to update or correct inaccurate information about themselves.

### Example

If a company has an old address or incorrect phone number, the person should be able to correct it.

---

# 5. Ability to Give Consent

People should be able to decide whether they agree to the collection or use of their data.

Consent should happen before the data is used.

---

# 6. Legal Right to Access Data

People should have legal rights to access their own data.

This means organizations should provide a way for individuals to view or request their personal data.

---

# Data Privacy for Companies

For companies, data privacy means putting protections in place to safeguard individuals’ data.

Companies are responsible for protecting data such as:

- names
- addresses
- phone numbers
- email addresses
- credit card information
- purchase history
- account information
- personal preferences
- online activity

---

# Why Data Privacy Matters

Data privacy matters because people need to trust organizations with their information.

Trust affects whether people are willing to:

- use a company’s product
- share their information
- create an account
- make a purchase
- interact with a service
- continue using a platform

### Important Lesson

Trust is a major responsibility for companies that collect and use data.

---

# Data Privacy and Government Laws

Governments around the world recognize the importance of data privacy.

Many have created data protection laws to help protect people and their data.

These laws help define how organizations should collect, store, share, and protect data.

---

# Data Privacy and Data Ethics

Data privacy is an important part of **data ethics**.

It connects to ethical ideas such as:

- consent
- ownership
- transparency
- fairness
- protection
- responsible data use

---

# Data Transactions

A **data transaction** happens whenever data is collected, accessed, used, shared, or processed.

Data privacy should be preserved during every data transaction.

---

# Examples of Data Privacy in Daily Life

## Shared Computer

Family members may want privacy when using the same computer.

Each person may expect that their files, browsing history, or messages are not accessed by others.

---

## Social Media Photo

A teenager may want to share a selfie with only specific people.

This involves controlling who can access and view the data.

---

## Customer Credit Card Information

A company must protect customers’ credit card information.

This requires security measures that prevent unauthorized access or misuse.

---

# Company Responsibility

Companies should create privacy measures to protect the data of individuals.

This can include:

- secure storage
- access controls
- encryption
- consent forms
- privacy policies
- employee training
- limited data access
- regular security checks

---

# Trust and Data Privacy

Trust is essential in data privacy.

When people trust a company, they are more likely to share data.

When companies misuse or fail to protect data, that trust can be damaged.

### Important Lesson

Data privacy is not only a technical issue. It is also a trust issue.

---

# Openness

The lesson also introduces another important part of data ethics: **openness**.

## Definition

**Openness** is the idea of free access, use, and sharing of data.

Openness is closely connected to data ethics because it raises questions about what data should be shared and how.

---

# Privacy vs Openness

Privacy and openness can sometimes create tension.

## Privacy

Focuses on protecting personal information.

## Openness

Focuses on making data available for access and use.

### Important Lesson

Ethical data use requires balancing privacy with openness.

---

# Big Lesson

Data privacy protects people’s personal information and gives them rights over how their data is accessed, used, collected, and shared.

Companies must take this responsibility seriously because people’s trust depends on it.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Privacy is personal, and everyone is entitled to it.
- Data privacy means preserving a data subject’s information and activity during data transactions.
- Data privacy is also called information privacy or data protection.
- Data privacy is about access, use, collection, and legal rights to data.
- People should be protected from unauthorized access to their private data.
- People should be protected from inappropriate use of their data.
- People should have the right to inspect, update, or correct their data.
- People should be able to give consent for how their data is used.
- People should have a legal right to access their data.
- Companies must put privacy measures in place to protect individual data.
- Governments have created data protection laws to protect people and their data.
- Trust is important because people need confidence that companies will protect their data.
- Openness is another part of data ethics and involves free access, use, and sharing of data.

---

# Extra Important Notes

- Data privacy applies whenever data is collected, used, shared, accessed, or processed.
- A data subject is the person the data is about.
- Companies have a responsibility to protect customer data.
- Data privacy helps prevent misuse of personal information.
- Strong privacy practices can help build trust between people and organizations.

# Final Summary

Data privacy is the protection of a data subject’s information and activity whenever data is collected, used, shared, or processed. It is also called information privacy or data protection. Data privacy gives individuals important rights, including protection from unauthorized access, freedom from inappropriate use, the right to inspect and correct data, the ability to give consent, and the legal right to access their data. Companies are responsible for putting privacy measures in place to protect individuals’ information, such as customer credit card data. Governments around the world have also created data protection laws to protect people and their data. The main lesson is that data privacy is essential to data ethics because it protects people, supports trust, and guides responsible data use.
