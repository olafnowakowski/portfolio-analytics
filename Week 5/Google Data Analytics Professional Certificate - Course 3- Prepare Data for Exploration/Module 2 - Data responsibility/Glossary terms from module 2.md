# Data Quality and Credibility Terms

## Good Data Source

A **good data source** is a data source that is:

- reliable
- original
- comprehensive
- current
- cited

This is remembered with the acronym **ROCCC**.

---

## Bad Data Source

A **bad data source** is a data source that does not meet the ROCCC standards.

It is not:

- reliable
- original
- comprehensive
- current
- cited

Bad data sources can lead to inaccurate analysis and poor decisions.

---

## ROCCC

ROCCC stands for:

- **Reliable**
- **Original**
- **Comprehensive**
- **Current**
- **Cited**

A dataset that meets all five standards is more trustworthy.

# Bias Terms

## Bias

**Bias** is a conscious or subconscious preference in favor of or against a person, group of people, or thing.

Bias can affect how people collect, interpret, and analyze data.

---

## Data Bias

**Data bias** happens when a preference in favor of or against a person, group, or thing systematically skews data analysis results in a certain direction.

Biased data can produce unfair or unreliable conclusions.

---

## Fairness

**Fairness** is a quality of data analysis that does not create or reinforce bias.

Fair analysis should avoid unfairly favoring or excluding people, groups, or outcomes.

---

## Sampling Bias

**Sampling bias** happens when certain members of a population are overrepresented or underrepresented because the sample is not representative of the population as a whole.

### Example

Surveying only people walking on the sidewalk to understand all commuters would exclude people who drive, bike, or take public transportation.

---

## Unbiased Sampling

**Unbiased sampling** happens when the sample being measured is representative of the population as a whole.

This helps make analysis more accurate and reliable.

---

## Confirmation Bias

**Confirmation bias** is the tendency to search for or interpret information in a way that confirms pre-existing beliefs.

### Example

An analyst may focus only on data that supports their original assumption and ignore data that challenges it.

---

## Interpretation Bias

**Interpretation bias** is the tendency to interpret ambiguous situations in a positive or negative way.

### Example

Two people may hear the same voicemail, but one person thinks it sounds angry while another thinks it sounds calm.

---

## Observer Bias

**Observer bias** is the tendency for different people to observe things differently.

It is also called **experimenter bias**.

---

## Experimenter Bias

**Experimenter bias** is the tendency for different people to observe things differently.

It is also called **observer bias**.

### Example

Two researchers may record different observations while looking at the same experiment.

---

# Data Ethics Terms

## Ethics

**Ethics** are well-founded standards of right and wrong that prescribe what humans ought to do.

Ethics usually involve:

- rights
- obligations
- benefits to society
- fairness
- specific virtues

---

## Data Ethics

**Data ethics** are well-founded standards of right and wrong that dictate how data is collected, shared, and used.

Data ethics helps guide responsible data practices.

---

## Consent

**Consent** is the aspect of data ethics that assumes individuals have the right to know how and why their personal data will be used before agreeing to provide it.

### Important Lesson

People should understand what they are agreeing to before sharing their data.

---

## Currency

**Currency** is the aspect of data ethics that assumes individuals should know about financial transactions resulting from the use of their personal data and the scale of those transactions.

### Important Lesson

People should know if their data is being used to create financial value.

---

## Transaction Transparency

**Transaction transparency** is the aspect of data ethics that assumes all data-processing activities and algorithms should be explainable and understood by the individual who provides the data.

### Important Lesson

People should be able to understand how their data is processed and used.

---

## Openness

**Openness** is the aspect of data ethics that promotes free access, usage, and sharing of data.

Openness supports the idea that data can be shared and reused when it is ethical and appropriate.

---

# Privacy and Protection Terms

## Data Privacy

**Data privacy** means preserving a data subject’s information any time a data transaction occurs.

Data privacy protects people’s personal information from unauthorized or inappropriate use.

---

## Data Anonymization

**Data anonymization** is the process of protecting people’s private or sensitive data by eliminating identifying information.

### Examples of data often anonymized:

- names
- phone numbers
- addresses
- medical records
- account numbers
- email addresses
- social security numbers

---

## Cookie

A **cookie** is a small file stored on a computer that contains information about its users.

Cookies can help websites remember user preferences or track activity.

---

## General Data Protection Regulation of the European Union

The **General Data Protection Regulation**, or **GDPR**, is a European Union data protection regulation created to help protect people and their data.

### Important Note

GDPR focuses on protecting personal data and giving individuals more control over how their data is collected and used.

---

# Data Access and Sharing Terms

## Open Data

**Open data** is data that is available to the public.

Open data can be accessed, used, reused, and shared, as long as it meets ethical and legal standards.

---

## Data Interoperability

**Data interoperability** is a key factor that supports the successful use of open data among companies and governments.

It refers to the ability of data systems and services to connect and share data.

### Example

Healthcare systems may need interoperability so hospitals, pharmacies, labs, and clinics can share patient-related information appropriately.

---

# Data Source Terms

## First-Party Data

**First-party data** is data collected by an individual or group using their own resources.

### Example

A company collecting survey responses directly from its own customers.

# Final Summary

Course 3, Module 2 introduces important terms related to bias, credibility, privacy, ethics, and open data. Good data sources follow the ROCCC framework: reliable, original, comprehensive, current, and cited. Bad data sources do not meet these standards. Important ethical concepts include consent, currency, transaction transparency, privacy, anonymization, openness, and interoperability. The main lesson is that Data Analysts must use credible data, reduce bias, protect privacy, and handle data ethically.
