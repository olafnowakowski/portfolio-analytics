# Internal and External Data

## Main Idea

Data Analysts need to know where data comes from and how to access it.

There are two basic types of data used by Data Analysts:

1. **Internal data**
2. **External data**

Both can be useful depending on the analysis question.

---

# Why Data Access Matters

There is a lot of data available, but analysts need to know how to access the right data for the task.

Data access helps analysts:

- answer business questions
- solve problems
- create deeper analysis
- support data-driven decisions
- add context to findings

---

# Internal Data

## Definition

**Internal data** is data that lives within a company’s own systems.

It is usually generated from within the company.

---

# Internal Data Is Also Called Primary Data

Internal data may also be described as **primary data**.

This is because the organization collects or generates the data itself.

---

# Examples of Internal Data Sources

Internal data may come from different company departments or systems, such as:

- sales
- marketing
- customer relationship management
- finance
- human resources
- data archives

---

# Why Gathering Internal Data Can Be Complicated

Gathering internal data can be complicated because data may be spread across many systems and departments.

A project may require data from several areas of the business.

### Example

A sales analysis might need:

- sales records
- marketing campaign data
- customer relationship data
- finance data
- archived historical data

---

# Advantages of Internal Data

Internal data has several advantages.

It is often:

- relevant to the company’s specific problems
- already owned by the company
- free to access
- useful for internal decision-making
- directly connected to business operations

---

# Why Internal Data Is Valuable

Internal data helps analysts work on business problems without needing to search outside the company.

It can provide strong insight into:

- customers
- sales
- employees
- finances
- business operations
- company performance

---

# Limitation of Internal Data

Internal data does not always give the full picture.

Sometimes, a company’s own data is not enough to fully understand a problem.

In these cases, analysts may need external data.

---

# External Data

## Definition

**External data** is data that lives and is generated outside an organization.

---

# External Data Is Also Called Secondary Data

External data is sometimes called **secondary data**.

This is because it comes from outside sources rather than from the company’s own systems.

---

# Sources of External Data

External data can come from many places, including:

- other businesses
- government sources
- media sources
- professional associations
- schools
- nonprofit organizations
- public databases
- partner organizations

---

# Why Analysts Use External Data

Analysts use external data when internal data does not provide enough information.

External data can help add:

- broader context
- industry-level perspective
- comparison points
- public trends
- market information
- outside evidence

---

# Healthcare Example

Healthcare analysts may partner with:

- other healthcare organizations
- nonprofits

They may use partner data to create deeper analysis and add more industry-level perspective.

---

# Internal Data vs External Data

| Data Type | Meaning | Also Called | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Internal data** | Data inside a company’s own systems | Primary data | Sales, finance, HR, CRM data |
| **External data** | Data generated outside the organization | Secondary data | Government data, media data, nonprofit data |

---

# Open Data

## Definition

**Open data** refers to free access, usage, and sharing of data.

Open data gives analysts access to many datasets that can be used for analysis.

---

# Open Data Initiatives

Open data initiatives make public datasets available for people to use.

These initiatives help make data more accessible.

---

# Data.gov

## Definition

**Data.gov** is a website where the United States government makes hundreds of thousands of datasets available to the public.

---

# Examples of Data.gov Topics

Data.gov includes datasets about:

- weather patterns
- educational progress
- crime rates
- transportation
- science
- research
- public services
- government activities

---

# Why Governments Share Open Data

Governments share open data for several reasons.

These include:

1. Improving transparency
2. Educating citizens
3. Improving public service
4. Supporting innovation and economic growth

---

# 1. Transparency

Open data can make government activities more transparent.

### Example

The public can see where government money is being spent.

---

# 2. Citizen Education

Open data can help educate citizens about:

- voting
- local issues
- public programs
- community needs

---

# 3. Public Service Improvement

Open data can improve public service by giving people ways to:

- participate in public planning
- provide feedback to the government
- understand community services

---

# 4. Innovation and Economic Growth

Open data can support innovation and economic growth.

It helps people and companies better understand their markets.

---

# Google Public Databases

Google hosts public databases with information on topics such as:

- science
- transportation
- economics
- climate

These databases can help analysts access external data for analysis.

---

# Example: Bike Sharing Company

## Scenario

A bike sharing company wants to decide where to place its bikes.

## External Data Used

The company could use traffic data from a public transportation database.

## Analysis Goal

The company could identify where roads are busiest.

## Business Decision

The company could place bikes in busy areas to:

- reduce cars on the road
- give people another transportation option
- improve access to bikes
- support better transportation planning

---

# Combining Internal and External Data

Internal and external data can be used together.

This can help analysts create a more complete analysis.

### Example

A company might combine:

- internal customer data
- external market data
- public transportation data
- government demographic data

---

# Why Combining Data Helps

Combining data can help analysts:

- see the bigger picture
- understand outside trends
- compare internal performance to external conditions
- create stronger recommendations
- make more informed decisions

---

# Importing Data

After collecting data from different sources, analysts may need to import it into a spreadsheet.

This allows them to:

- clean the data
- organize the data
- analyze the data
- compare sources
- create reports
- generate visualizations

---

# Big Lesson

Data Analysts use both internal and external data.

Internal data helps analysts understand what is happening inside the company.

External data helps analysts add broader context and understand what is happening outside the company.

Together, they can support stronger analysis and better decisions.

---

# Extra Important Notes

- Internal data may be enough for some projects.
- External data is useful when internal data does not show the full picture.

# Final Summary

This lesson explains where Data Analysts go to connect with data. There are two basic types of data: internal data and external data. Internal data lives within a company’s own systems and is usually generated by the company. It may come from departments such as sales, marketing, CRM, finance, HR, and archives. Internal data is useful because it is relevant and already owned by the company, but it may not always provide the full picture. External data lives outside the organization and can come from sources such as government agencies, businesses, media, schools, professional associations, nonprofits, and public databases. Open data, such as datasets available through Data.gov, allows free access, usage, and sharing of data. Open data can improve government transparency, public service, innovation, and economic growth. The main lesson is that analysts should know how to access both internal and external data and use them together when needed to create stronger, more complete analysis.
